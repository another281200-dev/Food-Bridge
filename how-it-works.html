How FoodBridge Connects Communities

From the commercial kitchen door to the family dinner table. Here is how our transparent, 3-step food recovery network operates every single day.

For Food Donors

Restaurants, bakeries, grocers, and catering teams.

  • 1. Post in 60 seconds: Enter food type, estimated weight, temperature, and pickup time.
  • 2. Keep food safe: Keep food in clean containers under appropriate holding temps until courier arrives.
  • 3. Tax receipts: Receive digital weight verification for IRS tax deductions under IRC § 170(e)(3).

For Food Pantries

Community shelters, soup kitchens, and distribution centers.

  • 1. Instant notifications: Set custom alert radius and receive alerts only for items you can use.
  • 2. Reserve with 1 tap: Claim the batch so the donor knows where it is heading.
  • 3. Intake check: Complete a simple 30-second temperature scan upon arrival.

For Couriers & Drivers

Neighborhood volunteers, cyclists, and community drivers.

  • 1. Pick local routes: Choose rescue missions that fit along your daily route or commute.
  • 2. Use thermal bags: Carry insulated totes provided by FoodBridge to maintain cold-chain safety.
  • 3. Immediate handoff: Deliver directly to the shelter intake coordinator within the 60-minute transit window.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are food donors legally protected from liability?

Yes. The Federal Bill Emerson Good Samaritan Food Donation Act (42 U.S. Code § 1791) provides comprehensive civil and criminal immunity to businesses and individuals who donate wholesome food in good faith to nonprofit organizations.

What foods can and cannot be donated?

We accept fresh produce, artisan breads and pastries, dairy, commercially packaged goods, and hot or chilled catering dishes that have remained in temperature-controlled holding. We cannot accept open customer leftovers, home-canned foods, or items with unverified temperature histories.

Does FoodBridge charge any fees?

No. FoodBridge is a 100% free community platform. Food donors and recipient pantries never pay membership fees or transaction costs. The initiative is supported by local civic grants and community contributions.
