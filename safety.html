Public Health & Legal Standards

Food Safety Standards & Donor Protection

Trust and safety are the bedrock of food recovery. We operate with medical-grade cold-chain standards and full federal liability protection.

Cold-Chain Temperature Rules

Perishable dairy and prepared dishes must stay below 40°F (4°C) during storage and transport. Hot-hold prepared meals are kept at 140°F (60°C) or above. Drivers record infrared readings at each handoff.

Clear Allergen Tagging

Every donation requires clear labels marking any of the major allergens (peanuts, tree nuts, dairy, eggs, fish, shellfish, soy, wheat). Items with potential cross-contact are clearly identified for shelter staff.

Good Samaritan Act Protection

The Federal Bill Emerson Good Samaritan Food Donation Act (42 U.S. Code § 1791) shields businesses, grocers, and restaurants from civil and criminal liability when donating surplus food in good faith to nonprofit organizations.

Federal Legal Protection: 42 U.S. Code § 1791

In 1996, President Bill Clinton signed the Bill Emerson Good Samaritan Food Donation Act to encourage food donation to nonprofit organizations. The law states:

"An apparently wholesome food or an apparently fit grocery product that is donated in good faith to a nonprofit organization for ultimate distribution to needy individuals shall not be subject to civil or criminal liability arising from the nature, age, packaging, or condition of apparently wholesome food."

Key requirement: The donor must not engage in gross negligence or intentional misconduct. Donating wholesome food nearing its "best by" date remains completely protected.
